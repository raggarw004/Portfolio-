personal project

Evolutionary Neural Architecture Search

01

Honestly, this one came out of pure laziness. I didn't want to sit there manually trying different numbers of layers and channel sizes, so I made the computer do the guessing for me. It starts with a bunch of random little network designs, trains each one on FashionMNIST, lets the best ones "breed," mixes and mutates their depth and channel sizes over a few generations, and slowly narrows in on something accurate that isn't bloated. Turns out a decent chunk of "model design" is really just trial and error dressed up nicely this just automates the trial and error part.

pytorchnasgenetic algorithm
personal project

Behavior Sequence Lab

02

This one started as a random question late one night: could you tell how someone's doing focused, tired, confused just from the way they move their mouse and type, without ever seeing their face? So I built something to test it. A bidirectional LSTM watches a rolling window of mouse and keyboard behavior and guesses the person's state in real time, with a smoothing layer so it doesn't flip flop every time you click a little weird. It's actually live behind a small FastAPI app with a big disclaimer, because this is a fun experiment, not a mental health tool.

pytorchbilstmfastapi

personal project

Cooperative Multi Agent PPO

03

I've always liked the idea of agents figuring things out on their own instead of me hardcoding rules for them. So here, I built a tiny grid world where a team has to split up and grab targets together, and trained them with a shared PPO policy instead of babysitting each agent separately. The tricky (and kind of fun) part was the reward too generous to the team and everyone gets lazy, too generous to individuals and they stop working together. By the end they were bumping into each other a lot less, which felt like a small win.

pytorchppomulti agent rl
personal project

AI Trading Signal Model

04

This one's basically me asking "can I actually predict anything about the stock market" and then spending way too long finding out. I combined an LSTM with a Transformer to catch both quick momentum and longer patterns in price data, and made sure to backtest it out of sample instead of trusting the training numbers, since that's usually where these projects quietly fall apart. Short answer: no, it doesn't reliably beat the market nothing simple does but building it and trying to break it taught me more about time series modeling than a semester of lectures would have.

academic project

Theme Park Decision Support Tool

06

For a business analytics class, my team got handed a business question that actually sounded real: should a theme park push sports drinks over fountain drinks in its ads? Instead of just guessing, we built an Excel tool that ran a Monte Carlo simulation basically a thousand simulated versions of the future to see how that decision would actually play out. We ended up recommending the campaign because the numbers backed it up, not because it sounded like a good idea, which taught me a lot about how far you can trust a spreadsheet if you set it up right.
